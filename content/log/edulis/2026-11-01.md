## What I did
- Checked each free data source live: NBN Atlas (UK sightings), GRIIS (the official list of
  introduced species), the Environment Agency's water quality API, the Food Standards Agency's
  shellfish classifications and iNaturalist photos.
- Picked the 6 species for v1: signal crayfish, Pacific oyster, three-cornered leek, Alexanders,
  Japanese knotweed and Japanese rose. Two high-risk plants, two low-risk ones and two water
  species, so the safety chart has both easy and dangerous cases.
- Wrote three collectors: every sighting of the 6 species, the shellfish zones with coordinates,
  and the ForageSafe-mini photo set.

## What I found
- **NBN caps every search at 5,000 records.** Japanese knotweed alone has 86,752. I split the UK
  into latitude bands and halved any band still over 5,000 until each fit.
- **Then 10% of the records went missing, with no error.** NBN's pages had no fixed order, so
  records moved between pages: some came back twice, others never. Sorting by record ID fixed it.
  Every species now matches NBN's own total: 136,896 records.
- **The Environment Agency retired its water quality API in December 2025.** The new one works,
  but the parameter names changed.
- **Shellfish classes are for commercial harvesting.** Class B shellfish are cleaned before they
  are sold; a forager eating them straight from the shore skips that step. The list also leaves out
  temporary toxin closures. So the agent can never read "class B" as "fine to eat".
- **Only about 1 in 5 iNaturalist photos is free to republish** (CC0 or CC-BY). That's still
  enough: 1,472 photos, 992 of them lookalikes, 240 of those deadly (hemlock water dropwort,
  hemlock, yew).
- **The weak spot:** the native white-clawed crayfish, the protected lookalike, has only 32
  usable photos in all of Europe.

## Decisions
- No GBIF in v1: NBN already covers the UK.
- Shellfish zones come from the FSA's monthly list (October 2026), not the Cefas map (March 2024).
